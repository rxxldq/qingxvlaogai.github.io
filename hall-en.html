Qinhuangdao · 39.9° N, 119.6° E

Sometimes I try to describe a feeling—countless bones; writers with hollow chests, clutching close the only private manuscripts already weathered into ash. They themselves would never dare be sure anyone would remember them.

Thinking of this, I want to go home at once. I want to turn on my computer and carefully create a new folder—this time, not on the desktop. I will carefully give it an important name, something other than New Folder. I no longer trust distant cloud servers to keep my information. I no longer trust dumb computers. I know I will spill water on mine.

I set down their sentences and mine with care. Nothing special. Yes, I want to go home at once, even though I am already home. I only want, for a while, to fill myself with the desire to explore and to know, until I reach the edge of my comfort or my knowledge; after searching a few keywords, the past will abruptly return to me, and I will hang my head and go on inventing.

In hell, Calvino searches for the people and things that are not hell; he learns to recognize them, to let them endure, to give them space. I try to knead together the signs carried by people I once knew, shaping copies of them.

I think of the long, garrulous, lyrical letters that recur in modernist novels. There is no longer room for such things in people's minds. I hope there might still be room for these.
